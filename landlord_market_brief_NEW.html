The questions I hear every day.

Cap Rate measures what a property earns relative to its purchase price or market value — it's a property metric. Return on Equity (ROE) measures what your money is actually earning given today's value and your remaining equity position. They're completely different numbers. Here's the trap: as your property appreciates, your equity grows — but your rent usually hasn't kept pace. So your ROE quietly shrinks every year while your Cap Rate looks stable. A landlord who bought at $300K and now holds a $1.2M property might feel wealthy, but their equity is earning 2–3% while the same capital in a better-yielding vehicle earns 6–7%. The appreciation created the illusion of performance while silently diluting real wealth.
California capital gains stacked on federal is 37.1% — among the highest in the country. But it's not the only option. A 1031 exchange defers the entire tax bill if you reinvest in another property. A Delaware Statutory Trust gets you out of management AND defers the tax. An installment sale spreads it across years. Section 121 wipes out up to $500K of gain tax-free if you ever lived in the property for 2 of the last 5 years. The full analysis shows you which one applies to your situation.
A good tenant is genuinely valuable. The question isn't whether the tenant is good — it's what your equity is actually earning. Most long-hold San Diego rentals earn 3–4% on today's value, with rents capped at 5%+CPI annually under California law. The same equity in a different vehicle could earn 5.5–7% with no tenants, no management, no California rent control. The tenant being good doesn't change the math on the equity.
Step-up basis is one of the best tax tools in the code — your heirs inherit at current market value and the capital gains get wiped out. The question worth asking: do they actually want to manage a rental? Most prefer simpler assets. The smarter wealth transfer might be redeploying the equity into something that fits their life — a DST that sends them a monthly check, or properties in a cash-flow market that earn more than this one ever will. Same family wealth, much cleaner inheritance.
Most agents calling you want a listing on one specific property. I work with landlords on the bigger question: what's the right strategy for your equity, given where the market actually is right now? Sometimes that means selling. Sometimes a 1031 exchange to a better market. Sometimes keeping the property and pulling equity out tax-free. The analysis I'd put together for you is a strategy document, not a pitch.
